Break-Even ROAS Formula & Mathematical Proof

In e-commerce advertising, Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) measures how much revenue your campaigns generate for every dollar spent. Your Break-Even ROAS is the threshold where marketing cost matches available unit margin:

Break-Even ROAS = Net Order Revenue / Pre-Ad Contribution Margin
Equivalently: Break-Even ROAS = 1 / (Contribution Margin % expressed as a decimal)
Break-Even CPA = Net Order Revenue - Total Non-Ad Variable Costs

Step-by-Step Worked Example

Consider a standard dropshipping product setup:

  • Retail Selling Price: $40.00
  • Supplier Product Cost (COGS): $15.00
  • Supplier Shipping / ePacket: $5.00
  • Credit Card Processing (2.9% + $0.30): $1.46
  • Refund & Loss Reserve (2.5%): $1.00
  • Platform Apps / Overhead: $0.54

Total Non-Ad Variable Cost: $15.00 + $5.00 + $1.46 + $1.00 + $0.54 = $23.00
Pre-Ad Contribution: $40.00 - $23.00 = $17.00
Contribution Margin: ($17.00 / $40.00) × 100 = 42.50%
Break-Even ROAS: $40.00 / $17.00 = 2.35x (or 1 / 0.425 = 2.35x)
Break-Even CPA: $17.00

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a break-even ROAS calculator?
A break-even ROAS calculator is an e-commerce unit economics tool that determines the exact return on ad spend (ROAS) required to cover 100% of your product sourcing, fulfillment, transaction fees, and shipping costs. At break-even ROAS, your net ad campaign profit is exactly $0.00.
How do you calculate break-even ROAS for dropshipping?
First, deduct supplier product costs, overseas freight, credit card processing fees, and refund reserves from your retail selling price to establish your pre-advertising contribution margin. Then divide total net revenue by this contribution margin.
What is the difference between break-even ROAS and target ROAS?
Break-even ROAS covers costs without yielding profit. Target ROAS includes a planned net profit margin (e.g., 15% or 20%), establishing the minimum ad performance required to hit your business profitability objectives.
How do I calculate break-even CPA?
Break-even CPA (Cost Per Acquisition) equals your net revenue minus all non-advertising variable expenses per order. It represents the maximum amount of money you can spend on paid ads to acquire a single paying customer before losing money on the order.
What is considered a "good" ROAS for a dropshipping store?
There is no single universal benchmark for a "good" ROAS. A store selling custom goods with an 80% contribution margin can be profitable at a 1.5x ROAS, while a store with a 20% margin will lose money at a 4.0x ROAS. Your required ROAS depends entirely on your product margin.
