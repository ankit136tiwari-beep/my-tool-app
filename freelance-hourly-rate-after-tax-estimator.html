Calculate Your Freelance Hourly Rate

Determining what to charge as a self-employed professional is one of the most critical business decisions you will make. When transitioning from salaried employment to freelancing, charging your former hourly wage usually results in a severe pay cut. As a sole proprietor or independent contractor, you must independently cover your own business operating expenses, health benefits, retirement contributions, and self-employment taxes—all while spending significant time on non-billable administrative tasks.

This freelancer take home pay calculator works backwards from your personal financial lifestyle requirements or forwards from your client quotes to ensure your freelance rate after taxes leaves you fully compensated.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Select your calculation mode: Choose Rate Finder (Mode A) if you have a target annual take-home salary in mind. Choose Take-Home Estimator (Mode B) if you want to see how much real income a specific hourly rate yields.
  2. Define your work schedule: Input realistic working weeks per year and your estimated billable utilization percentage. Most seasoned freelancers spend 25% to 40% of their total time on unpaid marketing, administrative overhead, invoicing, and client proposals.
  3. Account for business expenses: Enter software licenses, hardware amortizations, phone, internet, and office expenses as either a simple lump sum or an itemized breakdown.
  4. Input your estimated effective tax rate: Enter a realistic percentage reflecting national, state/provincial, and municipal taxes plus self-employment social contributions.
  5. Review your personalized dashboard: Evaluate the required hourly billing rate, gross invoicing milestones, and visual allocation curves.
