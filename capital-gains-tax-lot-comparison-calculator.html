Understanding Capital Gains Tax Lots and Disposition Strategies

When an investor purchases shares of a company, exchange-traded fund (ETF), or cryptocurrency across multiple dates, each transaction creates a distinct tax lot with its own cost basis and purchase date. When deciding to liquidate a partial holding, selecting which specific lots to dispose of dramatically influences the net realized gain or loss, holding period characterization, and resulting tax liability.

Key Lot Selection Strategies Compared

  • FIFO (First-In, First-Out): Sells the oldest acquired shares first. In rising markets, FIFO usually triggers larger capital gains because earlier purchase prices are often lower.
  • LIFO (Last-In, First-Out): Disposes of the most recently acquired units first. This frequently generates short-term capital gains, which are taxed at higher ordinary income rates in the US.
  • HIFO (Highest-In, First-Out): Sells units with the highest adjusted cost basis first, maximizing immediate capital losses or minimizing taxable gains.
  • LOFO (Lowest-In, First-Out): Sells lots with the lowest cost basis first. Useful when an investor wants to accelerate taxable gains (e.g., during low-income tax years).
  • Average Cost Basis: Divides total cumulative basis across all units. Primarily permitted for mutual funds and dividend reinvestment plans (DRIPs) under US IRC Treas. Reg. § 1.1012-1(e).
  • Specific Identification: Allows the taxpayer to instruct their broker exactly which designated certificates or lots to sell prior to settlement.

Holding Period: Short-Term vs. Long-Term

Under US tax law (IRS Publication 550), assets held for one year or less (365 days or fewer) generate short-term capital gains, taxed at marginal ordinary income rates up to 37%. Assets held for more than one year (at least 366 days) qualify for preferential long-term capital gains rates (0%, 15%, or 20%), plus an applicable 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT) for higher earners.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a capital gains tax lot comparison calculator?
It is an analytical financial tool that calculates realized gains, losses, and estimated taxes across different accounting methodologies (such as FIFO, LIFO, and HIFO) when selling securities acquired at different dates and prices.
How does FIFO affect capital gains?
First-In, First-Out sells your oldest acquired shares first. If the asset has appreciated over time, FIFO usually results in higher realized capital gains, though those gains are more likely to qualify for lower long-term tax rates.
What is specific identification of tax lots?
Specific identification is an IRS-recognized method where an investor specifies to their broker the exact lot (by purchase date and cost) to be sold prior to transaction settlement, providing maximum control over tax outcomes.
How do I compare FIFO with specific-lot selection?
By modeling both side by side in this calculator. FIFO sells chronologically, whereas specific identification allows you to selectively sell lots with the highest cost basis (HIFO) to reduce current tax liability.
What is adjusted cost basis?
Adjusted cost basis is the net original purchase price of an asset plus eligible acquisition commissions, purchase fees, and reinvested dividends, adjusted for corporate actions such as stock splits or returns of capital.
How are realized capital gains calculated?
Realized capital gain equals the Net Sale Proceeds (Gross Sale Price minus selling costs) minus the Allocated Adjusted Cost Basis of the specific shares sold.
What is the difference between realized and unrealized gains?
An unrealized ("paper") gain reflects the increase in value of an asset you still hold. A realized gain occurs when you execute an actual sale, closing the position and generating a taxable event.
How does the holding period affect capital gains tax?
In the United States, assets held for 365 days or less are classified as short-term and taxed at higher ordinary income rates. Holdings held for more than 365 days receive preferential long-term tax treatment.
Can I compare short-term and long-term capital gains?
Yes. The comparison table automatically splits realized gains into short-term and long-term buckets for each strategy, applying the appropriate tax rate to each category.
Does the lowest-tax strategy always provide the best financial outcome?
Not always. While HIFO minimizes immediate taxes today, it leaves your remaining portfolio with a lower average cost basis, potentially creating higher taxable gains in future sales.
Can this calculator compare cryptocurrency tax lots?
Yes. You can switch the asset class selector to Cryptocurrency. The tool handles fractional decimal units and evaluates cost basis strategies for digital assets under IRS Notice 2014-21 guidance.
Can I use the calculator for different countries?
Yes. You can select the UK, Canada, Australia, India, or Manual mode. When non-US jurisdictions are selected, the tool switches to a transparent manual rate estimation structure.
Does the calculator replace professional tax advice?
No. This calculator is strictly for educational and scenario planning purposes. Individual tax returns require considering carryover losses, alternative minimum tax (AMT), and wash sales with a licensed CPA.
Can I export my tax-lot comparison?
Yes. You can export your current lot inventory as a CSV file, download the strategy comparison metrics as a CSV, or use the Print button to generate a clean PDF summary.
Does this calculator upload my financial data?
No. All calculations, data parsing, and CSV processing occur client-side inside your browser. No financial amounts or lot records are transmitted to any server.
