How to Calculate 100Ah Lithium Battery Runtime

Determining how long a 100Ah lithium battery will power your devices requires evaluating usable energy rather than nameplate amp-hours alone. Because amp-hours measure electrical charge rather than work capacity, you must factor in nominal battery voltage, safe discharge limits, and electrical power path inefficiencies.

1. Nominal Energy (Wh) = Nominal Voltage (V) × Rated Capacity (Ah) 2. Usable Energy (Wh) = Nominal Energy × (DoD% / 100) × (1 - Reserve% / 100) 3. AC Battery Draw (W) = Appliance Watts / Inverter Efficiency 4. DC Battery Draw (W) = Appliance Watts (Zero Inversion Loss) 5. Estimated Runtime = Usable Energy (Wh) / Total Battery-Side Watts (W)

12V vs. 12.8V: The Lithium Energy Difference

Conventional lead-acid formulas use 12.0V as the baseline multiplier (12V × 100Ah = 1,200Wh). However, standard Lithium Iron Phosphate (LiFePO4) batteries utilize four 3.2V prismatic or cylindrical cells wired in series (4S configuration), resulting in a true nominal voltage of 12.8V.

This nominal voltage difference yields 1,280 Watt-hours—providing 80 additional Watt-hours (a 6.7% capacity bonus) over legacy estimates. Furthermore, while lead-acid batteries suffer severe voltage sag under Peukert's law, LiFePO4 maintains a flat 12.8V–13.2V discharge plateau across 80% of its cycle life.

Inverter Standby Draw & DC-Coupled Efficiency

A frequent cause of premature battery depletion is inverter tare loss. An inverter does not simply convert electricity; its internal transformers, cooling fans, and logic boards draw continuous standby power (typically 8W to 20W for pure sine wave units) whenever switched on.

When running a small 10W Wi-Fi router on an AC inverter with 15W tare loss, your battery experiences a 26W+ continuous draw—reducing runtime by more than 60%. Utilizing native 12V DC power connections bypasses both conversion losses and tare draws, maximizing total run time.

BMS Limits & High-Wattage Constraints

Even though a 100Ah battery stores over 1,150 usable Watt-hours, it cannot run every appliance. Standard 100Ah lithium batteries incorporate a Battery Management System (BMS) with a continuous discharge rating of 100 Amps.

At 12V nominal, this establishes a hard continuous power ceiling:

Maximum Continuous Power = 12.8V × 100A = 1,280 Watts

Attempting to run a 1,500W space heater, hair dryer, or large induction cooktop draws over 140 Amps at the battery terminals, triggering immediate thermal or over-current shutdown. Sizing an off-grid system for high loads requires pairing batteries in parallel to multiply allowable BMS amperage.
