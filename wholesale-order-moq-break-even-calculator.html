What this calculator does

The Wholesale Order Minimum Quantity Break-Even Calculator helps buyers, importers, resellers, manufacturers and distributors decide whether a minimum order quantity makes financial sense. It works out landed cost per unit, break-even quantity, bulk discount savings and order profit. Landed cost matters because freight, duties, taxes and storage can add much more to the real unit price than the supplier's quote shows. All values are entered by you.

How to use

  1. Select Buyer Mode or Supplier Mode.
  2. Enter your product and supplier information.
  3. Enter the supplier's minimum order quantity.
  4. Add product purchase or manufacturing costs.
  5. Enter shipping, freight, customs, packaging and handling expenses.
  6. Enter your expected selling price and sales quantity.
  7. Add applicable discounts and other expenses.
  8. Review the landed cost, break-even quantity and estimated profit.
  9. Compare different bulk order quantities.
  10. Download or print your report.

Formulas and examples

  • Landed cost = purchase cost + shipping and freight + customs and import charges + non-recoverable tax + storage and handling. Landed cost per unit = landed cost ÷ order quantity.
  • Contribution per unit = net selling price − variable cost per unit. Break-even quantity = fixed order costs ÷ contribution per unit, rounded up.
  • Target quantity = (fixed costs + target profit) ÷ contribution per unit.
  • Buyer break-even units = total investment ÷ contribution per unit sold (cash recovery view).

Illustrative example (assumed numbers): a buyer orders 500 units at $10 each. Shipping and other landed expenses are $500, so total investment is $5,500 and landed cost is $11 per unit. At a resale price of $18, selling all 500 units gives $9,000 revenue and $3,500 gross profit. This excludes selling expenses, returns, taxes or financing costs not entered. Cash break-even is 306 units (5,500 ÷ 18, rounded up).

MOQ versus break-even: MOQ is the smallest order the supplier will accept. Break-even quantity is the number of units you must sell to cover your costs. A supplier MOQ can be higher than your break-even quantity, which means you may carry unsold stock even when the order is profitable overall.

Use cases

Retailers buying from wholesalers, ecommerce sellers planning bulk purchases, small businesses evaluating supplier MOQs, importers estimating landed costs, distributors comparing bulk orders and resellers analyzing discounts can use the buyer mode. Manufacturers estimating production minimums and suppliers calculating minimum profitable order sizes can use the supplier mode.

Important notes and limitations

  • Supplier MOQs vary by supplier. Freight, customs and tax values must be entered manually. Nothing is fetched or verified.
  • Duty is calculated on purchase cost plus shipping and freight. Tax is calculated on purchase cost plus shipping and duty. Check your own jurisdiction's rules.
  • Recoverable tax is excluded from landed cost when you tick that option.
  • Discounts add together (base discount % + tier %) and are applied to the base price once. A negotiated price replaces the base price before discounts.
  • Returned units are assumed to go back into stock and refunded revenue is removed. The return cost per unit is your own entered loss.
  • Results are estimates, not financial, tax or legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is a wholesale minimum order quantity (MOQ)?

It is the smallest number of units a supplier will sell in one order.

How do I calculate wholesale break-even quantity?

Divide fixed order costs by contribution per unit, which is selling price minus variable cost per unit, then round up.

What is the difference between MOQ and break-even quantity?

MOQ is the supplier's minimum order. Break-even quantity is the sales volume you need to cover your costs.

How do I calculate the minimum profitable order quantity?

Find the smallest valid quantity where total revenue exceeds total costs. The calculator checks quantities in your pack and carton steps.

How do I calculate wholesale landed cost per unit?

Add product, freight, insurance, duties, non-recoverable taxes and handling, then divide by the units ordered.

How do bulk discounts affect wholesale profit?

They lower unit cost but raise upfront investment and inventory risk, so compare profit and unsold stock before ordering more.

How many units should I order from a wholesale supplier?

Order at least the MOQ and enough to be profitable, but stay within your budget, storage space and expected demand.

Can a supplier MOQ be higher than the break-even quantity?

Yes. Then you must buy more than you need to break even, so you may hold unsold stock.

How do I calculate wholesale order profit margin?

Divide net profit by net revenue and multiply by 100.

Can I use this calculator for both wholesale buyers and suppliers?

Yes. Buyer Mode and Supplier Mode each have their own inputs and formulas.
