What this calculator does

This free calculator shows what product returns really cost an online store. It separates order return rate from unit return rate, then works out refunds, reverse shipping, handling, write-offs, the value of resellable stock you get back, and profit before and after returns. It suits store owners, marketplace sellers, and analysts who want to see how returns affect profit, not just how many there are.

How to use it

  1. Pick Store-wide or Single product analysis.
  2. Choose reporting period and currency (labels only, no exchange conversion).
  3. Enter total orders and returned orders.
  4. Enter units sold and returned units.
  5. Add selling price and purchase cost.
  6. Enter refunds, or leave blank to calculate them from returned units.
  7. Add forward shipping and reverse logistics costs.
  8. In Advanced mode, describe the condition of returned stock and its recovery value.
  9. Switch on optional costs you actually pay.
  10. Read return rate, net return loss and profit after returns.
  11. Use the reduction simulator to estimate profit recovery.
  12. Copy, download, or print the report.

Features

  • Return rate analysis: order-based and unit-based, with annual projection.
  • Refund loss: manual or auto-calculated refunds, with payment, refund and chargeback fees.
  • Reverse shipping: cost per return with a recovered-by-customer or carrier percentage.
  • Inventory recovery: resellable, open-box, damaged and unsellable units, capped at cost.
  • Profit analysis: two accounting methods, break-even return rate and margin.
  • Product comparison for three products, a return reduction simulator, and what-if analysis.

Formulas and example

Order return rate = returned orders ÷ total orders × 100. Unit return rate = returned units ÷ units sold × 100. The two differ when orders contain several units, so they are never mixed.

Profit before returns = revenue − COGS − outbound shipping and packaging − marketing − other operating costs. Profit after returns = profit before − refunds − return costs (reverse shipping, handling, inspection, restocking, refurbishing, disposal, fees) + inventory recovery (method B only). Forward shipping and marketing are already in profit before, so they are not charged twice.

Net return loss = non-recovered forward shipping on returned orders + return costs + inventory write-off (+ marketing on returned orders if enabled). Refunds are not added again because they reduce retained revenue.

Worked example (the sample data): 1,000 orders and 120 returned; 1,500 units and 170 returned; price 40, cost 18. Order return rate 12%, unit return rate 11.33%. Revenue 60,000, refunds 6,800, COGS 27,000, forward shipping 5,000, marketing 4,000, so profit before returns is 24,000. Return costs total 1,170. Of the 170 returned units, 110 are resellable and 30 open-box (each restocked at cost, 18), 20 damaged (5 each) and 10 unsellable, so recovery is 2,620 and write-off is 440. Profit after returns (method B) = 24,000 − 6,800 − 1,170 + 2,620 = 18,650. Net return loss = 600 + 1,170 + 440 + 480 = 2,690, or 22.42 per returned order. Resellable goods are recovered at cost, not written off.

Use cases

Shopify and WooCommerce stores can paste totals from their order reports. Amazon sellers can include marketplace fees as other return costs. D2C brands and small online businesses can find which costs dominate. Multi-product sellers can compare products, and ecommerce analysts can model scenarios and export CSV.

Important notes and limitations

  • Results depend entirely on the values you enter; actual return costs vary by business.
  • A refund is not automatically a permanent inventory loss, because returned goods may be resold.
  • Tax, VAT, GST and accounting treatment may need separate calculation. Consult an accountant.
  • Inventory recovery is an estimate unless you enter actual resale values.
  • Annual figures are projections, not actual results.
  • This tool does not connect to Shopify, WooCommerce, Amazon, or any payment gateway. Entered data stays in your browser unless you choose to save a scenario locally.

Frequently asked questions

What is an ecommerce return rate?

It is the share of sales that customers send back, measured either by orders or by units sold.

How do I calculate ecommerce return rate?

Divide returned orders by total orders and multiply by 100 for the order-based rate. Divide returned units by units sold for the unit-based rate.

What is the difference between order return rate and unit return rate?

Order rate counts orders with a return. Unit rate counts items. If a customer returns one item from a three-item order, the order counts once and units count one of three.

How do I calculate the cost of product returns?

Add non-recovered forward shipping, reverse shipping, handling, inspection, restocking, refurbishing, disposal, payment fees and inventory write-offs. Subtract any costs recovered from customers or carriers.

How much profit does an ecommerce business lose from returns?

It varies widely with price, margin, shipping and product condition. Enter your own numbers: the calculator shows lost profit as profit before returns minus profit after returns.

How do refunds affect ecommerce profit?

Refunds reduce retained revenue. Profit falls by the refund amount minus whatever returned stock is worth when it is resold, plus the costs of processing the return.

How do I calculate reverse shipping costs?

Multiply returned shipments by the reverse shipping cost per return, then subtract any amount the customer, carrier or marketplace covers.

Are returned products always considered a financial loss?

No. Resellable items go back into stock at cost. Losses come from damaged or unsellable units, processing costs and lost margin on the refunded sale.

How can an online store reduce its return rate?

Improve product descriptions, photos and size guidance, check packaging and quality control, and look at which products or reasons drive returns. Then test the effect with the reduction simulator.

Is this ecommerce return calculator free and private?

It is free. Calculations run in your browser and nothing is sent to a server. Data is stored locally only if you click Save scenario, and you can delete it with Delete saved.
